maggie/about

MAGGIE is a printed magazine. It is a zeitgeist publication that works like a hate club for a nonexistent person; the person signifies what is rotting about scenes. Issues collect writing, photographs, emails, screenshots, horoscopes, conversations and reviews. See more here.

Each feature has its own layout in print, and keeps it here.

maggie/submissions

What is MAGGIE?

Did you hear what happened to Maggie? Someone saw her last week in a coffee shop in Santa Fe. Someone else swears she was at the show on Saturday. Everybody has a story about Maggie, and nobody ever invites her.

MAGGIE is everything but Maggie. It's the group chat she isn't in: people talking their shit, reviewing things, keeping diaries, forwarding emails, arguing about whatever everyone is consuming right now. Which, somehow, makes all of it about her.

Maggie doesn't have a face. She's less a girl than a mood, the spirit of the times the way people used to say the economy was sad today. You might be a Maggie. Some days we all are.

It isn't a zine; a zine has no purpose. If it's a bag, it's a mag. When you hear Maggie's been spotted somewhere, a new issue isn't far behind.

We want writing with a point of view, the opinion you'd defend at dinner, the kind of thing you'd only say about someone who isn't in the room.

What do the categories mean?

Reviews
Anything that can be reviewed: photography, music, film, products, events, services, a show, a shop, a sandwich. Say what it is, what it did to you and whether it was worth it. 500–1,200 words. Photos you took are welcome.
Journal
Diaries, notes-app entries, emails, letters, replies to your post. The running account of a life, dated if you can. Any length, from one line to a month. Screenshots or scans are fine; we will typeset or keep them as they are.
Photos
A series from one place or one stretch of time. 6–20 images at full resolution (JPG or TIFF), with the place and the date. Short video is welcome for the website.
Conversations
Two or more people talking: in a car, on the phone, over a drink, about anything. Record it (with everyone's yes), transcribe it, cut the boring bits. 1,500–4,000 words, plus a title and the date it happened.
Essays
Arguments about culture, aesthetics, technology, fashion, celebrity and why we feel the way we do about them. Talks you have given count. 1,000–3,000 words, with your sources at the end.
Horoscopes
A run of weekly horoscopes for one season: 6–12 weeks, 100–200 words each. Could be card readings you did for certain topics.

How can you send something?

Email info@meanwhilegettingmeaner.com with the category in the subject line and the work attached, or a pitch of a few lines if it isn't written yet. Word, Google Docs, PDF or the body of the email are all fine. Send big photo sets as a shared link.

You keep the rights to your work. We ask to print it in MAGGIE and show it on this site. Everything we receive gets a reply from a person, even if it isn't a yes this time.
